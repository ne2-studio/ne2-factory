# Structural and modularity smells

| id | Smell | Description | Attributes in tension | Candidate movements | Common false positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shotgun-surgery` | Shotgun surgery | One conceptual change forces edits across many modules, layers, services, or repositories. | `modifiability`, `cohesion`, `autonomy` vs. technical separation or reuse | Regroup by capability; vertical slice; move behaviour next to the data it governs; extract module; redraw the bounded context | A change that touches many files but each edit is mechanical and generated (e.g. a codemod) — the cost is one-time, not recurring |
| `divergent-change` | Divergent change | One module changes for several unrelated reasons — unrelated business rules, unrelated technical concerns, or unrelated feature logic all landing in the same file or class. Includes what's sometimes called low cohesion at the class level and Divergent Change at the module level; same pressure, different granularity. | `cohesion` vs. simplicity of having one component | Separate responsibilities; extract adapters; split the module; separate command from read model; modularise by reason to change | A file that's merely long, with every method still serving the same one responsibility |
| `cyclic-dependency` | Cyclic dependency | Two or more modules depend on each other, directly or through an intermediary. | `autonomy`, `testability`, `comprehensibility` vs. direct reuse | Dependency inversion; extract shared core; introduce events; merge the modules if they're genuinely one unit | A cycle formed only by test helpers or shared test fixtures, not production code |
| `hub-dependency` | Hub dependency | One central module accumulates both incoming and outgoing dependencies, becoming a mandatory coordination point. | `reusability`, `consistency` vs. `autonomy`, `modifiability` | Split the hub; decentralise responsibilities; extract contracts; replicate models where appropriate; introduce explicit mediation | A hub that's genuinely a stable, narrow, well-understood shared kernel with few real callers touching it often |
| `feature-scattering` | Feature scattering | One user-facing capability is spread across many components with no clear boundary owning it. | layered separation vs. `cohesion` by capability, `independent-delivery` | Vertical slicing; feature module; application facade; reorganise around use cases | A capability that's thin by nature (one field, one screen) and doesn't warrant its own boundary |
| `concern-tangling` | Concern tangling | One component simultaneously mixes business logic, persistence, authorization, observability, and transport concerns. | locality of the flow vs. separation of concerns | Extract policies; middleware/decorators; ports and adapters; pipelines; separate pure decisions from effects | Concerns that are tangled but always change together and have never varied independently |
| `distributed-monolith` | Distributed monolith | Independently deployable services still need synchronous coordination for almost every operation. | distribution overhead vs. `autonomy`, `resilience`, `modifiability` | Merge services; reduce synchronous calls; replicate data; events; sagas; redraw the boundaries | Synchronous calls that exist but are genuinely rare and non-blocking for the critical path |
| `modular-monolith-in-name-only` | Modular monolith in name only | Folders or projects look separated, but all of them reach into each other's tables, types, and internals. | visual organisation vs. `encapsulation`, `autonomy` | Internal visibility restrictions; public contracts; explicit data ownership; architecture tests; vertical modules | A shared type that's genuinely a stable, intentional public contract between the modules |
| `shared-kernel-creep` | Shared kernel creep | A shared core keeps accumulating types, utilities, and rules from multiple domains until it couples them all together. | `reusability` vs. `autonomy`, `domain-explicitness` | Shrink the shared kernel; accept conscious duplication; contracts package; anti-corruption layer; explicit ownership | A kernel that only holds primitives and utilities with no domain rules in them |
| `inappropriate-intimacy` | Inappropriate intimacy between modules | One module knows the internal structure, state, or private sequencing of another. | convenience or efficiency vs. `encapsulation`, `substitutability` | Facade; intention-oriented API; Tell-Don't-Ask; hide the representation; move behaviour to its owner | A caller reading genuinely public, documented state that the owner intends to expose |
| `context-scattering` | Context scattering | Understanding or safely changing one capability requires navigating far more files, hops, and searches than the capability's actual complexity warrants. | `context-efficiency`, `comprehensibility`, `modifiability` vs. genuine separation of independently-varying concerns | Co-locate what changes together; collapse forwarding layers; capability-oriented entry point; reduce representations of the same concept | Directory depth or file count alone, with no evidence anyone actually had to cross it to make a real change |
| `excessive-module-surface` | Excessive module surface | Consumers must learn and coordinate far more of a module's public shape — types, interfaces, configuration, sequencing — than the capability it provides would justify. | `comprehensibility`, `coupling`, `context-efficiency` vs. flexibility of exposing more | Capability-oriented API; collapse forwarding interfaces; hide intermediate state; merge fragments of one workflow into one operation | A facade that hides nothing — collapsing it would only rename the same surface, not shrink it |

## Notes

### `divergent-change`

This entry merges what used to be two separate criteria (`low-cohesion` and a module-level "divergent change"). They're the same underlying pressure — responsibilities that don't share a reason to change, living in the same place — observed at different scales. Report at whichever granularity the evidence actually shows; don't force a class-level finding up to module-level language or vice versa.

### `context-scattering`

Context efficiency is how much a maintainer — human or agent — must read, search, and hold in mind to understand or safely change one capability. Two codebases can be equally readable file by file while one requires reading four files to explain a feature and the other eighteen; only change-history evidence or a reconstruction exercise reveals that difference, not inspection of any single file.

The strongest evidence for this smell is a **representative maintenance task trace**: pick one real capability, walk it end to end from its public entry point, and record what the walk actually cost — e.g. "11 implementation files across 5 directories; 6 forwarding abstractions; 3 separate representations of the same status; 4 searches before locating the final decision; no single public contract describing the capability." This is demonstrable in a way that "this feels hard to follow" is not, and it is what `SKILL.md` Step 2's context reconstruction task is for.

Do not recommend co-locating code regardless of deployability, ownership, or runtime boundary; do not merge files solely to reduce file count; do not remove abstractions that genuinely isolate independent volatility; do not treat directory depth or file count alone as evidence.

### `excessive-module-surface`

Evaluate module depth as hidden useful complexity divided by exposed cognitive surface, in the sense of Ousterhout's deep modules — not as a strict formula, but as the mental model to apply. A module is not shallow merely because it spans several files or interfaces; it is shallow when the ratio of knowledge a consumer must hold to the capability actually delivered is high. A facade that still requires callers to understand the same internal collaborators, sequencing, or intermediate state has not reduced the surface — it has only renamed it.

A narrow facade over a stable, rarely-changing area can still be a valid initiative: the gain is not making the internals easier to change, it is letting the rest of the codebase stop needing to load them mentally.

Do not add a facade solely to reduce the visible number of classes while preserving the same consumer knowledge; do not merge modules merely because fewer files look simpler; do not hide independently useful capabilities behind a god service; do not collapse boundaries that vary, deploy, fail, or are tested independently.

### `inappropriate-intimacy`

The signal is not merely that callers access data — most objects expose some state, and that alone is not a violation. It applies when callers use exposed state to reconstruct knowledge about what the concept means, which operations are valid, or how its invariants must be preserved. A single occurrence can still qualify: the evidence is the reconstructed decision, not repetition of it.

The remedy is the smallest responsibility that removes the reconstruction, not a prescribed location. Depending on the evidence, this may surface as behaviour on the entity itself, a value object, a policy, a domain service, or an operation at the aggregate or module boundary. Do not default to "move it onto the entity" — identify the missing responsibility first, then let its owner fall out of what the decision actually depends on.

Do not move every conditional into an entity regardless of ownership; do not hide data merely to reduce the number of public properties; do not introduce getters and forwarding methods that preserve the same leaked knowledge; do not recommend this refactor solely as a general DDD preference.
