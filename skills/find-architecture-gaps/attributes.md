# Quality attribute catalogue

Every initiative reported by this skill must name which of these attributes it improves, and — since a design rarely improves one attribute for free — which ones it puts under tension.

This catalogue is the single source of truth for attribute names. Smell files reference attributes by `id`; do not invent new attribute names inline in a smell or in a report.

| id | Attribute | What it means | Improvement signals | Common tension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `modifiability` | Modifiability | Ease of changing existing behaviour without unexpected side effects. | Fewer components touched per change; changes stay local; fewer regressions. | vs. current simplicity — every seam that makes change safer is a seam someone must first understand. |
| `extensibility` | Extensibility | Ease of adding new behaviours or variants without rewriting what exists. | New cases added by extension, not by branching; conditionals stop growing. | vs. accidental complexity — extension points added before a second case exists are speculative. |
| `comprehensibility` | Comprehensibility | Ease of building a correct mental model of the system. | Clear names; traceable flow; predictable structure; less implicit knowledge. | vs. global abstraction — a locally simple story can hide a globally inconsistent one. |
| `context-efficiency` | Context efficiency | How much a maintainer — human or agent — must read, search, and hold in mind to understand or safely change one capability. Distinct from comprehensibility: a codebase can be clear file-by-file and still require reading eighteen files to explain one feature. | Fewer files/hops needed for a representative change; a stable entry point; less forwarding. | vs. separation of concerns — the files that minimise per-file complexity are not always the files that minimise total context. |
| `cohesion` | Cohesion | Degree to which responsibilities that change together live together. | Components with one dominant reason to change; less scattered co-change. | vs. component size — maximising cohesion can produce many very small components. |
| `coupling` | Coupling | Degree to which a component depends on another component's internal decisions. | Fewer dependencies; stable interfaces; changes that don't propagate. | vs. duplication or indirection — removing coupling by duplicating logic trades one cost for another. |
| `encapsulation` | Encapsulation | Ability to hide internal decisions and protect invariants. | Less direct access to internal state; less knowledge of internal representation required to use something correctly. | vs. convenience of direct access — encapsulation costs a caller a call instead of a field read. |
| `autonomy` | Autonomy | Ability of a module or subsystem to evolve, deploy, or operate independently. | Independent deploy, test, or change; less cross-team or cross-service coordination. | vs. global consistency — autonomous parts can drift apart. |
| `testability` | Testability | Ease of observing, controlling, and verifying behaviour — covers feedback speed, diagnostic quality, behavioural coverage, regression detection, and resistance to breaking on non-behavioural refactors. | Deterministic tests; small fixtures; failures localise to the broken decision; tests survive internal reorganisation. | vs. fidelity to the real environment — the easiest thing to test in isolation is not always the thing whose real-world behaviour matters. |
| `observability` | Observability | Ability to understand what is happening during execution. | Structured logs; useful traces; diagnosable errors; metrics that answer real operational questions. | vs. cost, noise, and privacy — everything logged is also everything that must be stored, scanned, and protected. |
| `robustness` | Robustness | Ability to keep correct behaviour in the presence of errors, unexpected state, or invalid input. | Failures are contained; invariants hold; recovery is explicit rather than accidental. | vs. simplicity and speed of the happy path. |
| `resilience` | Resilience | Ability to recover or degrade correctly from external or partial failures. | Controlled retries; idempotency; fault isolation; graceful degradation. | vs. immediate consistency — tolerating partial failure usually means tolerating a temporarily stale or incomplete view. |
| `consistency` | Consistency | Degree to which the system keeps rules, data, and behaviour coherent. | Fewer invalid states reachable; rules applied uniformly across entry points. | vs. availability or autonomy — enforcing one consistent rule everywhere usually means coordinating everywhere. |
| `efficiency` | Efficiency | Reasonable use of CPU, memory, network, storage, and time. | Lower latency; fewer calls; less redundant processing. | vs. clarity and isolation — the fastest path often skips the boundary that keeps things easy to reason about. |
| `scalability` | Scalability | Ability to absorb growth in load, data, or users. | Bottlenecks are identifiable; partitioning is possible; growth is predictable. | vs. operational complexity — most ways to scale add moving parts. |
| `domain-explicitness` | Domain explicitness & evolvability | How explicitly the model represents real business concepts, and how easily new domain knowledge can be folded into it. | Concepts are named, not implied by convention; rules are localised; ubiquitous language stays consistent as the domain grows. | vs. technical simplicity — a passive data bag is simpler than a value object until the rule shows up twice. |
| `reusability` | Reusability | Ability to use a component in several contexts without dragging in unnecessary dependencies. | Generic, stable APIs; few contextual dependencies. | vs. contextual cohesion — a component shaped for reuse is rarely the component shaped best for its one current caller. |
| `substitutability` | Substitutability | Ease of replacing an implementation, provider, or technology. | Dependencies sit behind a contract; migrations are bounded and mechanical. | vs. cost of the abstraction — every substitutable seam is a seam that must be designed and maintained before it's ever exercised. |
| `independent-delivery` | Independent delivery | Ease of building, verifying, and deploying small changes in isolation. | Vertical PRs; scoped pipelines; less cross-module or cross-team coordination to ship one change. | vs. global integration — the more independent the pieces, the more that must be verified once they meet. |
| `security` | Security | Ability to prevent unauthorised access, operations, and exposure. | Localised permissions; least privilege; smaller attack surface. | vs. usability and simplicity — most security boundaries cost the legitimate caller a step too. |
| `operability` | Operability | Ease of deploying, configuring, maintaining, and recovering the system in production. | Explicit configuration; viable rollback; repeatable procedures. | vs. architectural sophistication — the most operable design is often the most boring one. |

## Notes for the scout

* An initiative must name attributes it improves from this table by `id`. If nothing here fits, the initiative is not well-formed yet — go back to the evidence.
* Naming a tension is not optional decoration: an initiative that claims to improve `modifiability` for free, with no attribute under tension, has probably not been thought through — say what it costs, even when the cost is small.
* `Confidence` (how sure the report is that the evidence is real) is a property of the *report*, not an attribute of the codebase. It belongs in the output template's `Confidence:` field, not in this table.
